# Balance validation

Measured October 1, 2026 with `node app/scripts/balance.mjs 2000` (from `app/`: `npm run balance`), against the targets in [spec section 3.11](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-01-review-implementation-design.md#311-balance-targets-d1d6). Every journey is played through the real engine. Seeds are hashed (`seedFromText('balance-' + n)`, n = 1 … 2000), and every bot plays every background on the same 2,000 seeds. `app/tests/balance.test.js` repeats the measurement on 300 seeds with wider bands on every `npm test`.

## What changed and why

With the starting numbers and the same 2,000 seeds, Auto-buy alone won 76.3% of influencer journeys and 86.0–86.8% for the other backgrounds, against a target of 30–55%. Influencers won 10.5 points less than developers, beyond the 10 allowed, and the gap grew as passive play got harder (16 points in a trial at a 0.6 encounter chance). The careful bot already won every journey and the bot that never shops won none, so the work was to make passive play lose more often without hurting careful play much. Three numbers changed. `RULES.eventChance` went from 0.3 to 0.55, so the road meets an encounter on about half of all driving days rather than one in three. The heatwave's `damage` went from 6 to 14, so heat is a real hazard for a crew that never rests or eats well. The Wi-Fi outage's `influencerDamage` went from 8 to 5. That outage was the only thing that set influencers apart under Auto-buy, and it alone made the gap: at 3, like everyone else's, the gap fell to 1 point. Influencers still take more than the other backgrounds (5 against 3), so the joke stays. I looked at the road's wear first, but it is too coarse. The Steady wear and the Meager penalty each cost Auto-buy 17 health over 17 driving days per point, and one more point of either took Auto-buy from 86% to 10%. The encounter chance and the heatwave's damage can move in small steps. No price, starting value, or number without (T) changed.

## The bots

The bots choose from `availableActions` (by option key and whether it is enabled) and send everything through `transition`. The interface has its own controls for Auto-buy, pace and rations, which `availableActions` does not list, so the bots send those three actions to `transition` directly. To decide, a bot reads the state and the forecast, as a player reads the screen.

- **Never shops.** Drives at the default Steady pace on Meager rations and never opens a shop. When the tank is dry it trades the luggage, then pushes. It answers an encounter with the first enabled response that is open to every background.
- **Autopilot.** Auto-buy at every shop, Steady, Meager. It never rests, eats at the carts, forages, talks, or uses an item or the ability. It answers an encounter with the first enabled response that is not reserved for one background (`only`). When the tank is dry it trades the luggage, then pushes.
- **Careful.** Steady pace. In each shop it switches to Filling, then presses Auto-buy, so the plan buys food for Filling. Before each drive it picks Filling if the food lasts to the next shop (or Portland) with a day to spare, Meager if Meager does, and Bare otherwise. At a stop it talks and eats at the carts. While the crew's mean health is under 70, it rests at a stop that offers rest, at most three times per stop. It opens kombucha when anyone is sick or under 40 health. When food will not last at Meager, it scatters seed bombs, then forages if the crew's mean health is above 50. It uses its ability as a thoughtful player would. Collabs and salvage cost nothing, so it always uses them. A prepper scouts only when food is short and nobody is at 50 or below. A barista brews when someone is under 95 and the food covers the next stretch with two days to spare. It answers encounters with these preferences, best first, taking the first that is enabled:

  | encounter | preference |
  |---|---|
  | NFT fair | trade an NFT, audit (developer), wait |
  | food poisoning | treat, ride it out |
  | breakdown | repair, tow, kick |
  | outbreak | dose with kombucha, quarantine, drive through |
  | e-bike convoy | pour-overs (barista), crawl behind, honk |
  | Sasquatch | photo, leave it, chase |
  | toll | pay, ford (prepper), riddle |
  | brunch line | post (influencer), wait, detour |
  | petitions | call (developer), donate, sign |

  When the tank is dry it trades the luggage, then walks for fuel, then pushes.

"No bot may lose a journey in which every death was unavoidable" is checked through its stated case, the careful bot and the outbreak. Every other death in these runs follows from choices the bot made. Autopilot loses most outbreak journeys because it never heals afterwards. The bot that never shops loses because it never buys food or fuel.

## Note on careful play

Careful play has a wide margin. In scratch runs over 400 seeds it still won every journey with five alive when Filling's health was 1 or 0, or when the rests healed 8. Resting at stops while mean health is under 70 is enough to absorb what the road does. The targets set a floor for careful play, not a ceiling, so I left that margin as it is. Making careful play riskier would mean cutting healing (rests, Filling, kombucha). Those changes barely touch Autopilot, which never heals, so they could be tuned later without moving the passive-play numbers.
