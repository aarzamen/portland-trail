# Balance validation

Measured October 1, 2026 with `node app/scripts/balance.mjs 2000` (from `app/`: `npm run balance`), against the targets in [spec section 3.11](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-01-review-implementation-design.md#311-balance-targets-d1d6). Every journey is played through the real engine. Seeds are hashed (`seedFromText('balance-' + n)`, n = 1 … 2000), and every bot plays every background on the same 2,000 seeds. `app/tests/balance.test.js` repeats the measurement on 300 seeds with wider bands on every `npm test`.

**Status: one target is not met.** The careful bot's target, as amended, is 85–97% wins, at least 3.5 of 5 alive, and at least one traveler lost in at least 25% of journeys. Careful play still wins every journey with all five alive. Section "Why careful play cannot be made riskier with (T) numbers alone" shows why the (T) numbers cannot reach that target, and names the rule changes that could. Every other target holds.

## What changed and why

**Round 1.** With the starting numbers and the same 2,000 seeds, Auto-buy alone won 76.3% of influencer journeys and 86.0–86.8% for the other backgrounds. The target is 30–55%. Influencers won 10.5 points less than developers, beyond the 10 allowed, and the gap grew as passive play got harder. Three numbers changed:

- `RULES.eventChance` went from 0.3 to 0.55.
- The heatwave's `damage` went from 6 to 14.
- The Wi-Fi outage's `influencerDamage` went from 8 to 5. That outage was the only thing that set influencers apart under Auto-buy. At 3, like everyone else's, the gap fell to 1 point. At 5, influencers still take more, so the joke stays.

I looked at the road's wear first, but it is too coarse. The Steady wear and the Meager penalty each cost Auto-buy 17 health per point over 17 driving days. One more point of either took Auto-buy from 86% to 10%.

**Round 2.** The ruling asked for fewer, weightier encounters if a chance near 0.45–0.5 holds the bands, so `eventChance` went from 0.55 to 0.5 and the heatwave's `damage` from 14 to 16. Autopilot now wins 43.5–45.5% (it was 41.9–45.1%). Other pairs also hold the Autopilot band: 0.5 with a heatwave of 20 gave 42.5–44.0%, and 0.45 with 24 gave 45.3–47.8% (400 seeds each). I chose 0.5 with 16 because it holds the band with the smaller heatwave. No healing number changed, for the reason given below. No price, starting value, or number without (T) changed.

## The bots

The bots choose from `availableActions` (by option key and whether it is enabled) and send everything through `transition`. `availableActions` does not list Auto-buy, pace or rations, because the interface has its own controls for them, so the bots send those three straight to `transition`. To decide, a bot reads the state and the forecast, as a player reads the screen.

- **Never shops.** Drives at the default Steady pace on Meager rations and never opens a shop. When the tank is dry it trades the luggage, then pushes. It answers an encounter with the first enabled response that is open to every background.
- **Autopilot.** Auto-buy at every shop, Steady, Meager. It never rests, eats at the carts, forages, talks, or uses an item or the ability. It answers an encounter with the first enabled response that is not reserved for one background (`only`). When the tank is dry it trades the luggage, then pushes.
- **Careful.** Steady pace.
  - In each shop it switches to Filling, then presses Auto-buy, so the plan buys food for Filling.
  - Before each drive it picks Filling if the food lasts to the next shop (or Portland) with a day to spare, Meager if Meager does, and Bare otherwise.
  - At a stop it talks and eats at the carts. While the crew's mean health is under 70, it rests at a stop that offers rest, at most three times per stop.
  - It opens kombucha when anyone is sick or under 40 health.
  - When food will not last at Meager, it scatters seed bombs, then forages if the crew's mean health is above 50.
  - Collabs and salvage cost nothing, so it always uses them. A prepper scouts only when food is short and nobody is at 50 or below. A barista brews when someone is under 95 and the food covers the next stretch with two days to spare.
  - When the tank is dry it trades the luggage, then walks for fuel, then pushes.
  - It answers encounters with these preferences, best first, taking the first that is enabled:

  | encounter | preference |
  |---|---|
  | NFT fair | trade an NFT, audit (developer), wait |
  | food poisoning | treat, ride it out |
  | breakdown | repair, tow, kick |
  | outbreak | dose with kombucha, quarantine, drive through |
  | e-bike convoy | pour-overs (barista), crawl behind, honk |
  | Sasquatch | photo, leave it, chase |
  | toll | pay, ford (prepper), riddle |
  | brunch line | post (influencer), wait, detour |
  | petitions | call (developer), donate, sign |

## Why careful play cannot be made riskier with (T) numbers alone

The careful policy has a safety net. It opens kombucha whenever anyone is under 40. Auto-buy restocks two bottles at every shop, and `supplies.kombucha` is not a (T) number. It rests at stops while mean health is under 70. Between them, the weakest traveler stays at 30–40 or above. A traveler then dies only if one step takes about 40 health from them, and no encounter does that.

Careful play is also at least 17 health per traveler ahead of Autopilot before any healing counts. Filling must stay above Meager, so each of 17 driving days gives it at least one point more. Autopilot's crew is the same crew minus those points and minus every heal, and it loses someone in 67.5–71% of journeys.

Measured over 300 seeds per background, with careful play's policy unchanged:

| trial | influencer | dev | prepper | barista | outbreak journeys lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Moderate healing cuts: Filling 0, kombucha 5, every rest heal halved or less | 100% won, 0–1% lost anyone | same | same | same | 0% |
| Every healing lever at its floor: Filling 0; kombucha, meal, talk and every rest heal at 1 | 80.0% won, 33.0% lost anyone | 74.0%, 36.3% | 93.7%, 19.3% | 96.7%, 5.0% | 15 of 174 (8.6%) |
| The floor, plus doomscrolling 24 and treated berries 24 | 81.0%, 58.7% | 73.3%, 63.0% | 93.7%, 49.7% | 98.0%, 14.0% | 16 of 176 (9.1%) |
| The same, plus the barista's brew healing 2 instead of 5 (not a (T) number) | 81.0%, 58.7% | 73.3%, 63.0% | 93.7%, 49.7% | 85.7%, 43.0% | 20 of 177 (11.3%) |

Deaths in a quarter of journeys appear only when every heal is cut to 1. That breaks two other targets:

- The backgrounds spread 22.7 points apart.
- About 9% of outbreak journeys are lost, against at most 2%.

The barista is the outlier. "Brew coffee" heals 5 a day for 1 food. The careful barista brews 7–10 times a journey, so each traveler gets 37–48 extra health. No other background has a heal like it, and the ability's numbers are not (T). So the barista stays near 5% losing anyone while the other backgrounds pass 25%.

A broader random search covered 750 sets of (T) numbers. It drew the chance, the heatwave, heat per day, doomscrolling up to 40, treated berries up to 32, outbreak damage, Filling, kombucha, rest heals, meal, talk, Wi-Fi and sickness. No set reached 25% for the background that loses someone least often while holding the other careful targets.

The rule-level changes that would make the target reachable with sensible numbers, for the owner to choose from:

1. **Make the barista's brew weaker.** Heal 5 → 2, or once every 2 days. This needs the ability's numbers to be tunable.
2. **Thin the safety net.** For example: one rest per stop visit; Auto-buy stocks 1 kombucha instead of 2 (`supplies.kombucha`); or a rest that heals but leaves sickness. Each of these needs a rule or a number outside (T).
3. **Change the target.** For example, measure risk by how low the weakest traveler falls, not by deaths. With the current numbers the careful crew's weakest traveler bottoms out at 20–90 health, so that target would also need tuning.
